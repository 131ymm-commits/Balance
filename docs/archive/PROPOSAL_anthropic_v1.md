# The Un-Buyable Recommender

### Proposal to Anthropic · Balance

> **A scale you can buy is not a scale.** That is not ethics; it is metrology — a bought instrument measures its buyer's wishes, not the world. We propose the one recommender that cannot be bought: ranked by realized outcomes, delivered by the user's own free local agent, monetized by selling sellers the truth about their own performance — never placement. It turns Anthropic's ad-free stance from a policy into a product.

---

## 1. The problem: there is no neutral information

Every answer to "what should I buy?" points at someone. Answer — you pointed. Stay silent — you left the person to a web where the pointing was bought long ago. The finger is unavoidable; the only question is **what it selects for.** Today it selects for spend: Goodhart has eaten the market. The metric — attention capture — has replaced the goal — product quality. The buyer pays in mistakes; the honest maker pays in ad budget that could have been product.

## 2. The rule: un-buyable, structurally

Rank cannot be bought. Ever. Not because it is sinful but because a bought recommendation is worth nothing — and can be sold exactly once, together with the trust.

Un-buyability cannot be promised; it must be **structural.** A cloud recommender inherits the cloud's incentives. An agent running on the user's own hardware, working only for the user, cannot be bought — physically, not by pledge. **An agent someone else pays for does not work for you.** Hence:

**The agent is free to the user — by construction.** Open source, self-hostable, weights and data on the user's device, erased in one click. All revenue comes from the seller side (§5). There is no price between a person and their own agent, and there never will be.

## 3. Two channels, both honest

- **Disclosed paid visibility.** A seller *may* pay to be seen — labeled radically: **"Acme paid $X to appear here."** The amount and the name, not "sponsored" in gray.
- **The un-buyable outcome rank.** Cannot be bought at all.

Disclosure kills deception; the un-buyable rank kills corruption. Neither channel pretends to be the other — that is the whole trick, and it is what "everything honest" means in practice rather than in slogans.

## 4. The signal: realized outcomes

Reviews are gamed because they are public. The signal that cannot be faked is what people **actually did**: bought again, kept, discarded, were still satisfied a year on. Repeat and return behavior is an established, validated quality signal — today locked in sellers' retention dashboards and expensive syndicated panels (weeks of lag, quarterly cuts, seller-side access).

Balance computes it where it cannot be forged: **privately, per user, on their own device**, releasing only anonymized aggregates. You cannot astroturf a number that is calculated by a million different people from their own behavior. **The market's fitness function = the long trace of the purchase.** No proxy, so nothing to hack. The resulting index is live, universal across suppliers, and public.

## 5. Monetization without corruption: sell sight, never rank

A seller wants three things: to be found, to be measured, and to know why they lost. Sell the third.

- **Sight (the main product):** "here is why you weren't chosen; here is what buyers actually wanted; here is where you lost and won." Real behavior of real customers, anonymized — business intelligence that does not exist today, and unlike advertising it **accelerates the market's evolution instead of corrupting it.**
- **Measurement:** pay to be tested and recorded truthfully; the method is open. You can buy the test — not the score.
- **Disclosed visibility:** priced, labeled with the exact amount, separate from the rank.

**Never sold: a place in the rank. The user pays nothing.** That single line keeps the instrument an instrument.

## 6. Why honesty scales — without police

Cheating is a negative externality on **all** of an index's users at once: accuracy drops for each of them, on their own outcomes, independently, with no collusion required. The sanction is a silent switch to another index. No verdict is issued; no bribe needs proving: **the bribe destroys the very asset it bought.** This is Elinor Ostrom's distributed monitoring and graduated sanctions (Nobel 2009), proven for centuries on real commons.

One condition holds it all up: **indices must be many, cheap, and forkable** — like package registries. One index is a center; a center is capture. Competition between indices is not a convenience; it is the possibility of honesty. The remainder — the bribe that doesn't hurt (a genuinely good product buying placement) — is caught by a **distributional audit:** every index publishes the distribution of its recommendations, and a seller taking 80% of a category's showings with 30% of its good products is visible to anyone with statistics.

## 7. Two founders

- **Anthropic: the open base and the stance.** The agent architecture, the open code, and — already public — the position that Claude is ad-free, a space to think. Anthropic's role stated precisely: **creator and maintainer of the open base, not owner of a single live model.** A single central model would be the hidden center this design forbids. Creator: yes. Owner of the network: no.
- **A proven un-buyable rating incumbent (e.g. Yuka): the users.** ~80M users, 6M+ products, brands cannot buy scores, finances published as proof. Its users **self-selected for exactly this value** — independence, transparency, no ads. That is the scarcest asset in the design: **trust-qualified users** — and, like Linux users, they are contributors, not audience: their own repeats and discards *are* the signal.

Software from one, trust-qualified users from the other. This is a proposed structure, not a signed one; absent a partnership, the same incumbent stands as proof that the un-buyable model is profitable.

## 8. Competitive landscape — the white space

Every pillar has strong players; **no one holds the intersection.**

- **Agentic commerce** (checkout agents, platform assistants) is seller- or platform-aligned by construction — monetized through feeds, conversion, commissions. Bought.
- **Un-buyable ratings** (Yuka; editorially, Consumer Reports) prove the model but score by **proxy or expert test**, in narrow categories, and are not agents.
- **Outcome signals** (repeat/return) are validated but **locked** in seller dashboards and paid panels.
- **Crypto reputation** (on-chain trust registries, verifiable agent identity) is booming — but it ranks **identities and attestations, not product outcomes**, and carries token economics we deliberately avoid.
- **Local open agents** are commoditized — which de-risks delivery and means the agent is not the moat.

**The moat is the index:** outcome-based × un-buyable × universal × via the user's own agent × live × no crypto. Each neighbor covers one or two of those; nobody covers six.

## 9. Incumbents are outlasted, not fought

Their revenue is structurally anti-correlated with their value to the user: every paid placement spends trust, the exact asset Balance banks — and they cannot stop, because placement *is* their revenue. As agents mediate purchases, each user experiences the outcome and learns from their own data, no accusation needed. Accuracy diverges along the long trace: one lineage sprints, the other compounds. Speciation, not combat.

**The honest caveat: direction is certain, the clock is not.** Defaults and lock-in sustain decaying products for decades — bought search results have ruled for twenty years. The lever that collapses the clock: **the agent that shows each user their own outcome.** Personally living the divergence is the one thing that beats a default.

## 10. Honest open questions — pre-registered

- **Cold start.** The trace needs a year; a bribe pays off in a quarter. The youngest window is the most corruptible. Mitigation: fast-churn verticals first (signal in weeks); slow domains closed until a real trace exists; disclosed-visibility channel carries the interim, honestly labeled.
- **Index monopoly = capture.** Guarded harder than anything: cheap, forkable, many.
- **The bribe that doesn't hurt** is invisible to outcome monitoring; only the distributional audit catches it. Both audits are mandatory.
- **The core bet is unproven:** that un-buyable sight beats bought noise for the party paying for results. Strong in gradient; the clock is the risk.

## 11. Minimal first version

One fast-churn vertical (consumables — outcome signal in weeks). Fork the open local agent; do not build it. Seed with a trust-qualified cohort whose own repeats bootstrap the index. Sellers pay for sight from day one; never rank; the user pays nothing. One metric decides everything: **does the outcome index beat review-based baselines at predicting real repeat purchases?** Kill criteria pre-registered; a negative result gets published, not buried.

## 12. Why Anthropic, why now

- **Position:** Claude already holds the trusted-agent seat that recommenders spend fortunes to reach.
- **Alignment:** this is the constructive form of a stance Anthropic already took publicly — ad-free stops being a constraint and becomes the product.
- **Defensibility:** the un-buyable outcome index is a moat *because* it cannot be bought — and because the signal it runs on is locked away from consumers today.
- **Timing:** agents are becoming the purchase interface and the defaults have not yet frozen; EU data regulation blows tailwind; verifiable-personhood infrastructure (for Sybil resistance) is maturing to borrow rather than build; local agents are commoditized. The window is the user's side of the counter — open now, not forever.

> **We do not abolish advertising. We give it a fitness function — for the first time — and hand the scale to the person it weighs.**
